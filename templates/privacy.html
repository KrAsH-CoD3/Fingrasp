01Overview

Fingrasp is a browser-fingerprint intelligence platform. When you visit this website, your browser automatically communicates certain technical attributes to our servers. We collect and store a structured snapshot of those attributes — a device fingerprint — to power the core functionality of this service.

This policy explains precisely what we collect, why we collect it, and what we do (and do not do) with that information. We are committed to full transparency because we believe you have a right to understand every technical decision made about your data.

02What We Collect

On every page load, Fingrasp collects the following signals from your browser and device:

SignalExamplesSensitivity
Browser metadataUser-agent, language, timezone, DNT flagLow
Hardware hintsCPU cores, device memory, screen resolution & color depthLow
Graphics fingerprintWebGL renderer, vendor, canvas hashMedium
Audio fingerprintAudioContext oscillator output hashMedium

We do not collect names, email addresses, passwords, payment details, or any other directly identifying personal information unless you voluntarily provide them.

03Why Device Fingerprinting?

🔬
Our core mission is to advance the scientific understanding of browser entropy — how uniquely identifiable devices actually are across the modern web. Every fingerprint submitted to Fingrasp contributes to an anonymised research dataset that we analyse to measure fingerprint stability, collision rates, and the effectiveness of privacy-enhancing browser technologies.

We collect device fingerprints for the following specific, legitimate reasons:

🛡️

Fraud & Bot Prevention Research

Fingerprinting is one of the most reliable signals for distinguishing human visitors from automated bots. By building a large corpus of real-device fingerprints, we study how fraud-prevention systems can become more accurate and less invasive — ultimately reducing the need for disruptive CAPTCHAs and friction-heavy verification flows that affect legitimate users.

📊

Device Uniqueness & Entropy Analysis

We are actively studying how many bits of entropy different browser signals contribute to a composite fingerprint. This research informs browser vendors, standard bodies (W3C, WHATWG), and privacy tools like Tor Browser and Brave on which APIs leak the most identifying information — helping them make smarter privacy-preserving decisions.

🔁

Fingerprint Stability Tracking

A meaningful research question is: how long does a fingerprint remain stable as browsers update, OS patches ship, and hardware drivers change? We track fingerprint hashes over time — without linking them to personally identifiable information — to build stability models that benefit the entire security research community.

🌐

Session Continuity Without Cookies

As third-party cookies are phased out across the web, websites will need privacy-respecting alternatives to provide coherent user experiences (e.g., keeping a shopping cart across tabs). Fingrasp investigates whether probabilistic fingerprint matching can serve this purpose without storing personally identifiable information — a potential win for both usability and privacy.

✅
The bottom line: your fingerprint is treated as an anonymous technical signal, not as a profile of you as a person. It is never combined with your name, email, or any other directly identifying data unless you explicitly provide those separately.

04How We Use Your Data

  • Generating and storing a SHA-256 composite fingerprint hash for research purposes.
  • Analysing aggregate entropy across our dataset to understand browser uniqueness distributions.
  • Publishing anonymised, aggregated research findings — individual fingerprints are never published.
  • Improving the detection accuracy of our fingerprint collection pipeline.
  • Internal product analytics (e.g., which browser signals contribute most entropy).

We do not:

  • Sell your fingerprint data to any third party.
  • Use it for targeted advertising.
  • Link it to your name, email, or social profiles.
  • Share raw fingerprint records with any government entity unless compelled by a lawful order.

05Data Storage & Retention

Fingerprint records are stored on secure, encrypted servers and are protected using industry-standard technical and organisational measures. All data is encrypted at rest and in transit. Access is strictly restricted to authorised personnel via role-based access control.

Raw fingerprint records are retained for a maximum of 24 months from the date of collection. After that, records are either permanently deleted or irreversibly aggregated into statistical summaries that cannot be traced back to any individual device.

06Your Rights

Depending on your jurisdiction you may have the right to:

  • Access — request a copy of your fingerprint record.
  • Deletion — request erasure of your fingerprint record.
  • Portability — receive your data in a machine-readable format.
  • Objection — object to the processing of your data.

Because fingerprints are not linked to personal identifiers, to exercise these rights you will need to provide your fingerprint hash (visible on the main page) so we can locate your record. Contact us at the address below.
