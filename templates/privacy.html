01Overview & Scope

This policy describes the data collection practices of Fingrasp, a dedicated research tool for browser fingerprinting. When you interact with this application, your browser communicates specific technical attributes to our server. We process and store a structured snapshot of these attributes—a device fingerprint—to evaluate browser uniqueness and stability.

Our goal is to be fully transparent about the technical signals we record. This document provides a detailed breakdown of our data lifecycle, from the moment of collection to archival.

02Data Points Collected

Fingrasp records the following technical metrics from your browser environment:

Signal Category Parameters Sensitivity
Environment metadata User-agent, language preference, timezone offset Technical
Hardware profile CPU core count, memory estimate, screen dimensions Technical
Render signature WebGL renderer, GPU vendor, Canvas-based hashes Unique
Audio pipeline AudioContext oscillator characteristics Unique
Network context Masked IP address, connection type (4G, WiFi, etc.) Identifying
Composite fingerprint A one-way SHA-256 hash of all combined signals Primary ID

We do not collect PII such as names, physical addresses, email accounts, or financial data.

03Research Purpose

Anonymity by Design: Every fingerprint is treated as a technical data point, not a personal profile. Raw signals are never correlated with real-world identities or contact information.

Our research focuses on the following domains:

System Security & Bot Detection

By analyzing thousands of genuine device fingerprints, we study patterns that help security systems distinguish between legitimate human users and automated scripts, reducing the friction of bot-prevention tools.

Entropy Measurements

We measure the "uniqueness" of specific APIs. This data is shared with the security community to help browser vendors understand which features might unintentionally leak identifying data.

Fingerprint Stability

We track how often fingerprints change after OS updates or browser patches. Understanding the lifespan of a fingerprint helps in evaluating the reliability of device-based session management.

04Data Processing

Data processing is limited to the following actions:

  • Anonymized aggregation of browser entropy across the entire dataset.
  • Stability analysis to measure signal drift over time.
  • Development of refined detection logic for identifying spoofed platforms.

We do not sell data, use fingerprints for advertising, or share records for commercial gain.

05Security & Retention

Fingerprints are stored on encrypted infrastructure with role-based access controls. We adhere to strict data minimization: records are kept only as long as necessary for research validity. Once the research lifecycle concludes, data is irreversibly archived as high-level statistics.

06User Data Rights

Depending on your location, you may have legal rights to access, delete, or limit the processing of your technical data. To exercise these rights, you must provide your fingerprint hash (displayed on the homepage) so we can locate your record in our anonymous database.
